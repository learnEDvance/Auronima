# Auronima — Unified Fractal Object Space

## Core Shift

The system no longer treats the textbook as the only object capable of unfolding.

Instead, every learning object may possess an internal structure that can be explored.

Books remain the primary entry point into learning, but they are no longer unique in their ability to reveal deeper structure.

Understanding emerges by exploring increasingly connected structures rather than navigating isolated documents.

---

# Universal Object Model

Every learning object exists in one of two states:

* Compressed
* Expanded

Objects transition naturally between these states during exploration.

This interaction is universal.

Every object may be explored regardless of its type.

Examples include:

* Book
* Chapter
* Selection
* Concept
* Video
* PDF
* Question
* Experiment
* Historical Story
* Analogy
* Research Resource

The learner only needs to understand one interaction:

**Expand an object to explore what exists within it.**

---

# Object-Specific Unfolding

Although every object follows the same exploration principles, different objects reveal different kinds of internal structure.

Books unfold into:

* Pages
* Selections

Videos unfold into:

* Timeline events
* Timestamps

PDFs unfold into:

* Pages
* Sections

Experiments unfold into:

* Procedure
* Observations
* Conclusions

Questions unfold into:

* Hints
* Misconceptions
* Solutions

Concepts may unfold into:

* Analogies
* Prerequisites
* Applications
* Future abstractions

Expansion is universal.

The geometry revealed is unique to each object.

No two object types are required to unfold identically.

---

# Fractal Exploration

Exploration follows the same principles at every level.

An expanded object may contain additional objects.

Those objects may themselves expand.

Each newly expanded object temporarily becomes the center of its own local exploration space.

There is no distinction between "book exploration" and "object exploration."

The same recursive interaction model applies everywhere.

---

# Progressive Unfolding

Expansion does not necessarily reveal an object's complete internal structure.

Objects unfold only as much as the current exploration requires.

The primary textbook may unfold extensively because it serves as the foundation of exploration.

Other objects often reveal only the portions relevant to the learner's current context.

Additional structure appears naturally as understanding deepens.

This prevents overwhelming the learner while preserving continuity.

---

# Continuous Environment

There is only one continuous exploration environment.

The learner never switches between separate worlds.

Instead, exploration continuously shifts attention from one expanded object to another.

Previously explored objects remain present whenever they are still relevant.

Their representation changes naturally as exploration progresses.

---

# Dynamic Context

At any moment, one expanded object becomes the active context.

Its internal structure becomes the learner's immediate environment.

Previously expanded objects remain part of the larger space.

They simply become inactive until exploration returns to them.

Understanding therefore grows continuously rather than through disconnected screens.

---

# Local Coordinate Systems

Every expanded object establishes its own local coordinate system.

Its internal progression defines a local temporal reference.

Objects attached to one of its internal states inherit that state's position.

They do not exist independently of it.

For example:

Video

↓

Timestamp

↓

Question

↓

Analogy

↓

Experiment

If the learner moves to another timestamp, every object attached to that timestamp moves together with it while preserving their own internal relationships.

This behavior applies recursively.

Every expanded object temporarily becomes the origin of its own local exploration space.

---

# Relative Temporal Layers

Every expanded object possesses its own progression.

The currently active state always occupies:

Z = 0

States that have already occurred move toward negative Z.

States that have not yet occurred move toward positive Z.

Examples include:

* Earlier timestamps within a video
* Future timestamps
* Previous document sections
* Upcoming document sections
* Earlier experiment stages
* Future experiment stages

Z therefore represents progression relative to the currently explored object.

It is never a global coordinate.

Each expanded object defines its own local temporal layers.

---

# Inherited Relationships

Objects inherit the context of the state from which they emerge.

Children remain attached to their parent's internal state rather than occupying independent positions.

As the parent transitions through its progression, every descendant transitions with it.

Entire branches therefore move together while preserving their own local organization.

This creates stable, continuous exploration without requiring separate navigation systems.

---

# Persistent Exploration

Objects that have already been explored remain available.

If they remain relevant to the current context, they continue existing within the environment.

Their representation may become compressed or inactive, but their exploration state is preserved.

Nothing is recreated unnecessarily.

The learner experiences continuity rather than repeatedly opening and closing disconnected views.

---

# Primary Learning Object

Each exploration session begins from a primary learning object.

In most cases, this is a textbook.

Although every object may unfold, the primary textbook remains the visual and conceptual anchor of the exploration.

Objects discovered farther from this origin gradually decrease in visual scale.

Scale therefore communicates conceptual distance from the learner's starting point rather than importance.

The learner always retains an intuitive sense of how far exploration has progressed from its foundation.

---

# Fractal Consistency

Every expanded object temporarily behaves like a smaller version of the larger exploration space.

It establishes:

* its own progression
* its own local context
* its own relationships
* its own internal exploration

These same rules apply recursively at every level.

Books unfold.

Videos unfold.

Concepts unfold.

Experiments unfold.

Questions unfold.

Documents unfold.

No object type requires a fundamentally different interaction model.

The learner learns one exploration principle and applies it everywhere.

---

# Design Goal

The interface should maintain a continuous exploratory experience.

Learning should feel less like navigating software and more like uncovering structure that already exists.

Objects reveal relationships.

Relationships reveal further objects.

Every expansion preserves context while opening new paths for exploration.

Instead of moving between isolated views, the learner explores a single connected environment where every object may unfold into deeper structure without losing continuity or orientation.

Understanding emerges naturally through connected exploration.
